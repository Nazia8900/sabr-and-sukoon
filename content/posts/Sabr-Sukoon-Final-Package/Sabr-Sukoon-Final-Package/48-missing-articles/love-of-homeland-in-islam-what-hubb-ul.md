Hubb-ul-Watan: What Love of Homeland Actually Looks Like in Islam
A closer look at homeland, belonging, and what the Prophet ﷺ actually taught us about loving a place.
💡 Content Integrity:
This article separates a popular statement about love of homeland from authenticated Prophetic reports. Where a statement is not established as a hadith, it is clearly identified rather than attributed to the Prophet ﷺ.
By
Nazia Firdous
· The Sukoon Seeker · Sabr and Sukoon
📌 In Short:
Loving the place where you were born or raised is a natural human feeling. But the famous phrase
“Hubb-ul-Watan min al-Iman”
— “Love of homeland is part of faith” — should not be presented as an authentic hadith of the Prophet ﷺ.
  This article separates what is authentically established from what is popularly repeated, and explores
  what love of homeland can look like when it is rooted in responsibility, honesty, and care for others.
Why Does Homeland Feel So Personal?
There are some places we carry with us even after we leave them.
A street where we grew up. A familiar morning sound. The smell of food made at home. A language that feels effortless. The people whose presence makes a place feel like ours.
That emotional attachment is part of being human. So when Pakistan's Independence Day arrives and people decorate their homes, raise flags, remember their history, and speak about their country with affection, it is natural to ask:
Does Islam have anything to say about this love?
There is, however, one important correction to make first.
“Hubb-ul-Watan min al-Iman” — Is It Really a Hadith?
The Arabic phrase
Hubb-ul-Watan min al-Iman
means:
“Love of homeland is part of faith.”
It is often shared as though these were the words of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. But that attribution is not established.
⚠️ Important Hadith Note
The phrase
“Hubb-ul-Watan min al-Iman”
is not established as an authentic hadith from the Prophet ﷺ. Some hadith scholars have classified it as fabricated, while others stated that they could not find an established prophetic source for it.
That distinction matters. A statement can express a sentiment that people find beautiful without being a hadith. We should not turn a popular saying into a Prophetic quotation simply because its message sounds appealing.
In fact, being careful about what we attribute to the Prophet ﷺ is itself part of respecting his Sunnah.
But Did the Prophet ﷺ Love a Place?
Yes — and this is where the subject becomes especially beautiful.
We do not need the popular phrase to prove that attachment to place is human. There are authentic narrations showing the Prophet's ﷺ affection for Makkah and his desire for Madinah to become beloved to him and the believers.
اللَّهُمَّ حَبِّبْ إِلَيْنَا الْمَدِينَةَ كَحُبِّنَا مَكَّةَ أَوْ أَشَدَّ
“O Allah, make Madinah beloved to us as we love Makkah, or even more.”
— Sahih al-Bukhari 1889
Think about what this means.
The Prophet ﷺ had left Makkah — the place deeply connected to his early life — and was now living in Madinah. He asked Allah to place love for his new home in their hearts.
That is a remarkably human dua.
“This Is a Mountain That Loves Us”
There is another well-known authentic statement about Mount Uhud.
“This is a mountain that loves us and we love it.”
— Sahih al-Bukhari 2889
This narration does not establish the popular statement about homeland being part of faith. But it does beautifully demonstrate that affection connected with a place is not foreign to the Prophetic tradition.
A mountain became associated with people, memories, sacrifice, and belonging. The words are simple, but the feeling behind them is powerful.
What Can We Actually Say About Love of Homeland?
A careful Islamic approach would be something like this:
Natural attachment to one's homeland is human.
People naturally feel connected to the places where they grew up and the communities they know.
The famous phrase is not an authentic Prophetic hadith.
Therefore, it should not be quoted as though the Prophet ﷺ said it.
Authentic narrations do show affection for places.
The Prophet ﷺ prayed for love of Madinah and expressed affection connected with Makkah and Uhud.
Love must remain within Islamic ethics.
Loving a country does not justify injustice, hatred, arrogance, discrimination, or supporting wrongdoing.
Loving Your Country Without Losing Your Principles
There is a difference between loving a country and believing that everything done in its name is automatically right.
A Muslim can love their homeland and still recognize its problems. They can appreciate its people while wishing for better education, justice, safety, honesty, and opportunities. They can celebrate its beauty without pretending that every part of its history is perfect.
Real love does not always mean blind praise.
Sometimes love looks like responsibility.
Sometimes it means teaching children to care for their surroundings, treating neighbours well, doing honest work, protecting public spaces, helping someone in need, and refusing to contribute to corruption or harm.
What Could Love of Homeland Look Like in Everyday Life?
If we want to turn affection into something meaningful, we can begin with ordinary actions.
1. Serve rather than simply celebrate.
Love becomes meaningful when it benefits other people.
2. Protect what belongs to everyone.
Keeping streets, schools, parks, and public spaces clean is a small but real form of responsibility.
3. Speak truth with kindness.
Caring about a country does not require pretending that its problems do not exist.
4. Make dua for its people.
Instead of only posting about a homeland, remember its people in your prayers.
On Pakistan Independence Day
For Pakistanis, 14 August naturally brings memories, flags, family conversations, school activities, national songs, and reflections on the country's journey.
Whatever form one's celebration takes, it is worth remembering that the deepest expression of love for a homeland is not limited to decoration or words.
It can also be found in honesty.
In kindness.
In teaching children responsibility.
In caring for neighbours.
And in making sincere dua for the people and future of the country.
A Moment to Sit With
You do not need to call a popular saying a hadith to justify caring about your homeland.
    The authentic Sunnah already gives us something more beautiful:
    a Prophet ﷺ who asked Allah to place love for a new home in the hearts of its people.
Final Sukoon Reflection
Maybe the most meaningful way to think about
hubb-ul-watan
is not to ask, “Can I prove my love with a famous quote?”
Instead, ask:
“What does my love make me do?”
Does it make me kinder?
More responsible?
More honest?
More concerned about the people around me?
If so, then perhaps love of homeland has become more than a slogan. It has become a responsibility.
And perhaps that is a better message to carry with us than a beautiful sentence we cannot authentically attribute to the Prophet ﷺ.
Frequently Asked Questions
Is “Hubb-ul-Watan min al-Iman” an authentic hadith?
No. The phrase is not established as an authentic hadith from the Prophet ﷺ. Some hadith scholars classified it as fabricated, while others said they could not find an established prophetic source for it. It should therefore not be quoted as a hadith.
Did the Prophet ﷺ love Makkah?
Yes. Authentic Prophetic reports show his affection for Makkah and his attachment to places connected with his life. He also asked Allah to make Madinah beloved to them as Makkah was, or even more.
Did the Prophet ﷺ show affection for Madinah?
Yes. In Sahih al-Bukhari 1889, he asked Allah to make Madinah beloved to them as Makkah was, or even more, and asked Allah to bless it.
Is loving your country allowed in Islam?
Natural affection for one's homeland is human and does not itself conflict with Islam. The important boundary is that love of country should not become an excuse for injustice, arrogance, hatred, or supporting wrongdoing.
