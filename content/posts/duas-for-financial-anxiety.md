## Words for the Nights You Lie Awake Doing Math

🤲 Hands raised in dua, seeking provision from Allah
Sabr And Sukoon

There's a specific kind of tired that comes from money worry. It's not just the bills. It's the 2am math, the guilt of asking for help, the quiet shame of feeling like you should have this figured out by now. If that's where you are tonight, this post isn't going to hand you a five-step budget plan. It's going to hand you words — the exact words the Prophet ﷺ and the Prophets before him turned to when rizq felt tight.

These aren't superstition or shortcuts. Dua is not a replacement for effort — it sits alongside it. You still apply for the job, still make the budget, still have the hard conversation. But you also say these words, because Allah is Ar-Razzaq, the Provider, and He has told us to ask.

### 📌 Key Takeaways

- Dua for provision works alongside effort (asbab) — not instead of it.
- Musa (AS) made one of the most quoted duas for need while a stranger in a foreign land (Quran 28:24).
- The Prophet ﷺ taught a specific dua for being crushed by debt (Sahih al-Bukhari).
- A dua for being made independent of everyone except Allah is recorded in Jami' at-Tirmidhi.
- Consistency in these duas matters more than reciting them perfectly once.

## 1. The Dua of Musa (AS) — For When You Have Nothing Left

01رَبِّ إِنِّي لِمَا أَنزَلْتَ إِلَيَّ مِنْ خَيْرٍ فَقِيرٌRabbi inni lima anzalta ilayya min khayrin faqeer"My Lord, indeed I am, for whatever good You would send down to me, in need."Quran — Surah Al-Qasas 28:24Musa (AS) said this after fleeing Egypt with nothing — no home, no money, no plan. He didn't ask for a specific amount. He simply admitted his need before Allah, and help arrived within the same passage. Say this when you don't even know what to ask for, only that you need help.وَمَن يَتَّقِ اللَّهَ يَجْعَل لَّهُ مَخْرَجًا وَيَرْزُقْهُ مِنْ حَيْثُ لَا يَحْتَسِبُ"And whoever fears Allah — He will make for him a way out, and will provide for him from where he does not expect."Surah At-Talaq — Quran 65:2-3

## 2. The Prophet's ﷺ Dua Against Debt and Anxiety

02اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْهَمِّ وَالْحَزَنِ، وَالْعَجْزِ وَالْكَسَلِ، وَالْجُبْنِ وَالْبُخْلِ، وَضَلَعِ الدَّيْنِ وَغَلَبَةِ الرِّجَالِAllahumma inni a'udhu bika minal-hammi wal-hazan, wal-'ajzi wal-kasal, wal-jubni wal-bukhl, wa dala'id-dayni wa ghalabatir-rijal"O Allah, I seek refuge in You from anxiety and grief, from incapacity and laziness, from cowardice and miserliness, from being overcome by debt and overpowered by men."Sahih al-BukhariThis dua was taught by the Prophet ﷺ himself, unprompted, to his companions. Notice it doesn't separate financial stress from emotional stress — debt and grief are named in the same breath. Say this in the morning and evening, especially during a season of debt.

## 3. The Dua for Being Made Independent Through the Halal

03اللَّهُمَّ اكْفِنِي بِحَلَالِكَ عَنْ حَرَامِكَ، وَأَغْنِنِي بِفَضْلِكَ عَمَّنْ سِوَاكَAllahumma-kfini bi halalika 'an haramik, wa aghnini bi fadlika 'amman siwak"O Allah, suffice me with what You have made lawful, against what You have made unlawful, and make me independent of all others besides You by Your bounty."Jami' at-TirmidhiThis is the dua for the moment you're tempted to cut corners — a shady loan, an interest-based fix, a shortcut that doesn't sit right with your deen. It asks Allah for enough halal provision that haram never has to look tempting again.
> 💡 **A note on effort:** None of these duas are meant to replace budgeting, job-searching, or asking for help from people around you. The Sahaba who recited these duas were also the ones out trading, farming, and working. Dua opens doors; it doesn't replace walking through them.

## 4. The Dua of Yunus (AS) — For When You Feel Trapped

04لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا أَنتَ سُبْحَانَكَ إِنِّي كُنتُ مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَLa ilaha illa anta subhanaka inni kuntu minaz-zalimeen"There is no deity except You; exalted are You. Indeed, I have been of the wrongdoers."Quran — Surah Al-Anbiya 21:87Financial hardship can feel like being swallowed whole — by bills, by comparison, by shame. This is the dua Yunus (AS) made from inside the whale, and Allah says in the next ayah that this is how believers are saved from distress. Say it when you feel like there's no way out.

## 5. Istighfar — The Quiet Key to Rizq

05فَقُلْتُ اسْتَغْفِرُوا رَبَّكُمْ إِنَّهُ كَانَ غَفَّارًا \* يُرْسِلِ السَّمَاءَ عَلَيْكُم مِّدْرَارًا \* وَيُمْدِدْكُم بِأَمْوَالٍ وَبَنِينَFaqultustaghfiru rabbakum innahu kana ghaffara... yumdidkum bi amwalin wa banin"I said, 'Seek forgiveness from your Lord, indeed He is ever a Perpetual Forgiver... and provide you with wealth and children.'"Quran — Surah Nuh 71:10-12This is a shorter mention here because we've covered istighfar and barakah in more depth in a separate post — but it belongs on this list. When nothing else seems to be working, increasing istighfar is itself a means Allah names directly in the Quran for opening up rizq.

## What This Looks Like in Real Life

You don't need to recite all five today. Pick one — the one that matched something you felt while reading — and say it after your next prayer. Let it become a quiet habit rather than a one-time cry for help. Pair it with one practical step: send that job application, make that budget, have that hard conversation you've been avoiding.

Rizq in Islam was never meant to be chased alone. It was meant to be asked for, worked for, and trusted to arrive — often from a direction you never expected.

## Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best dua for financial anxiety?There isn't a single "best" dua, but the dua of Musa (AS) in Surah Al-Qasas 28:24 — "My Lord, indeed I am, for whatever good You would send down to me, in need" — is one of the most cited for financial need, alongside the Prophetic dua seeking refuge from anxiety and debt found in Sahih al-Bukhari.

Is there a specific dua for debt in Islam?Yes. The Prophet ﷺ taught a dua for being overwhelmed by debt: "Allahumma-kfini bi halalika 'an haramik, wa aghnini bi fadlika 'amman siwak" — "O Allah, suffice me with what You have made lawful, against what You have made unlawful, and make me independent of all others besides You." It is recorded in Jami' at-Tirmidhi.

How often should these duas be recited?There's no fixed number attached to these duas. Many people recite them after the five daily prayers or during times of financial stress, alongside sincere effort (asbab) and tawakkul — reliance on Allah.
